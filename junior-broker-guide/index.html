The Deal Stack

Every funded deal passes through all seven layers. Qualifying work lives at the top. Broker work owns the middle — learning to own those middle layers is the whole preparation. Tap a layer.

Qualifying a deal vs. owning a deal

Qualifying — the foundation

Finding opportunities, making first contact, and checking the basics: is there real equipment, a real amount, and a real timeline? These skills don’t go away — they’re the base a broker builds on.

  • Ask complete discovery questions
  • Take notes someone else could act on
  • Spot a weak or fake deal early

Owning — the Junior Broker skill set

Carrying a deal from qualified to funded: structuring it, reading credit the way a credit reviewer reads it, handling objections without giving away the deal, and getting documents right so funding isn’t delayed.

  • Quote a structure, not just a payment
  • Pre-screen credit before submitting a file
  • Negotiate to a yes and stop there
The prep gap = middle of the stack

Discovery opener — talk track

“Thanks for reaching out — so I point you at the right structure, can I ask four quick things? Who’s the business on paper and how long have you been operating? What exactly are you looking to finance, and how many? What total amount and down payment did you have in mind? And when do you need the unit working?”

Then listen. Write down only what they actually say.

Structuring — talk track

“There are three levers on the payment: how much you put down, how long we spread it, and what’s left at the end. Longer lowers the payment but costs more overall. More down lowers both. Let’s look at the trade-off that fits how long you’ll keep the unit.”

Credit conversation — talk track

“Before this goes to a credit reviewer, I want to make sure the story holds together — how long in business, whether you’ll personally stand behind it, and where the down payment comes from. Anything that might look weak on paper, I’d rather we name it now and explain it than have it found later.”
Closing talk track to learn: “Based on what you told me — [equipment] by [date] at $[amount] — here’s the structure that gets you there: [term] months, $[down] down, payment around $[X]. If that works, the next step is getting the credit review started so the timeline holds. Does that work?” Then stop. The scenario ends at the yes.

Capstone — work a practice deal end to end

The proof you’re ready isn’t a test score. It’s working one complete practice scenario through every layer without it bouncing back — the same discipline a real funded deal demands.

Pick the right practice scenario

Core equipment, a realistic business, a real timeline. Not the biggest scenario — the cleanest one you can work completely.

Discover it completely

Who / what / how much / when, plus PG, down, and TIB — before you touch structuring or credit.

Structure before you quote

Term matched to equipment life, down that helps credit, payment the business could carry on a slow month. Show the trade-offs.

Pre-screen like a credit reviewer

Run Lab 3 on your practice file. Surface the weak spot yourself, with a mitigant — don’t wait for a reviewer to find it.

Close to a clean yes

One structure, one clear next step, confirmation. No extra options after the yes.

Assemble a funding-ready file

Practice the documents layer: names matching everywhere, exactly, with title and insurance requirements identified early — not at the deadline.

Review it and learn from it

Write notes a future broker could re-run the deal from. What slowed it down? That’s your next module to re-read.

Glossary — plain words

FMV Lease
Fair Market Value lease — lower payment, at the end you return, buy at market value, or extend. Good for equipment that ages fast.
$1 Buyout / Capital Lease
The customer owns it at the end for $1. Payment is higher than FMV, but the asset is theirs. Common for trucks/trailers kept long-term.
TRAC Lease
Terminal Rental Adjustment Clause — common for titled vehicles; end value is agreed up front with a split of sale proceeds.
EFA
Equipment Finance Agreement — a loan secured by the equipment. The customer owns it, the lender holds a lien.
Personal Guarantee (PG)
Owner personally backs the deal. If they won’t sign, a credit reviewer hears “I don’t believe in this either.”
Time in Business (TIB)
How long the business has operated under current ownership. Under 2 years = startup pricing / more conditions, almost everywhere.
UCC Filing
Public lien filing on the equipment. Prior UCCs tell a credit reviewer who else has a claim.
Credit Package
The file assembled for a credit decision — business, owner, equipment, and financials, put together accurately. Learning to build a clean one is a core broker skill.
Floorplan
Dealer inventory financing — a dealer borrows to stock units and repays as they sell. A product to understand, with its own risk logic.
Residual / Balloon
Amount left at the end of term. Lowers the payment, raises the end decision. Never hide it in a quote.
DSCR
Debt Service Coverage Ratio — cash flow vs. all payments. Below 1.0× means the business doesn’t cover its debts on paper.
Vendor Finance
Financing offered at the dealer/vendor point of sale. Speed and dealer trust win deals rate alone doesn’t.
