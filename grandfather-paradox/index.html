Grandfather Paradox

Go back. Stop him. Watch yourself disappear.

Open the app → See how it works

What it does

It starts with a photograph: your whole family on the wall of the bahay, you front and center. Every face in that frame had to happen — exactly the way it happened. Then the app hands you a time machine.

You drop into a Philippine town in the 1950s — bahay houses, a kalesa, the sari-sari store — and come face to face with your Lolo at nineteen. He has your face. He shakes your hand. Then he turns toward the store where Lola works, and you get one choice: stop him. The moment you do, the photo back home starts losing faces, one by one — and yours fades last. Then the paradox lands: if you vanish, nobody went back, so he meets her, so you're born, so you go back.

The town flickers between both timelines until you pick an escape: a fixed timeline that protects itself, branching worlds that spare you, or a paradox that simply never resolves. It takes about ninety seconds, one hand on your phone, and it ends with a question.
