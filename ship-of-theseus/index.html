Ship of Theseus

Is it still the same ship?

Open the app →

What it does

Swap the planks of a ship one at a time. Keep every old plank. When the last one is replaced, build a second ship out of the old ones. Then decide which ship is the real one.

So the question is simple. If every part of something gets replaced, one at a time, is it still the same thing? And if it isn't, which plank was the one that made the difference?

The story comes from Plutarch, a Greek writer of the first century AD. In 1655 the English philosopher Thomas Hobbes made it worse: he imagined the old planks reassembled into a second ship. The app ends exactly there, and explains the main answers people give.

How it works

  1. Swap the planksOne at a time, with the slider or the buttons. Every old plank is kept.
  2. Answer as you goIs it still the same ship? Answer at any point. Then keep swapping.
  3. Meet the second shipWhen the last plank is replaced, the old ones become a second ship. Decide which is the real one.
