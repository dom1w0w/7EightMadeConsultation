The Board

Every computer is one switch, copied billions of times.

Open the app →

What it does

It starts with a question Dom asked one night: what's the thing on the board that works like a transformer — and what are transistors, really? This app is the long answer, built so you can touch it.

The Board lays out a real motherboard — the example rig's, carrying an Intel i7-13700KF, an RTX 4060 Ti and 32 GB of DDR5 — and lets you tap every component: the CPU socket, the RAM, the chipset, the capacitors, and the VRM, the part that steps 12 volts down to the ~1.2 a processor can survive. Each one gets the same three answers, in plain language: what it is, why it exists, and what breaks if it dies.

And now you don't just tap it — you build it. A build mode hands you the empty board and the parts tray: seat the CPU, click the RAM home, plug the cooler's fan in, then press power and discover whether your build boots or beeps at you. Every classic first-build mistake is in there waiting. So is a VRM you work by hand, a logic-gate challenge with actual goals, and an overclocking dial with a glitch for a conscience.

Then it follows the power from the wall to the chip, and zooms — board, CPU, inside the chip — down to a single transistor you switch on and off yourself. A slider runs the fifty-year shrink from the 10-micrometre transistors of 1971 to the 3-nanometre ones in a modern phone. Two switches wired as logic gates close the loop: decisions, built from nothing but on and off.

How it works

  1. Explore the boardTap any of the fourteen parts for its story — including what dies with it.
  2. Follow the power120 volts at the wall become ~1.2 at the CPU. Watch the VRM do the delicate part.
  3. Build it, boot itAssemble the rig part by part in build mode, then press power — and meet the beep codes if you got it wrong.
  4. Climb down the ladderBoard → CPU → die → one transistor. Flip its gate and make a 1 out of a 0, then run the great shrink.

One switch, copied billions of times. Go flip it.
