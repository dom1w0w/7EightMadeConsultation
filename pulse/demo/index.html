PULSE.

Your phone camera is the sensor. Cover the rear camera with your fingertip, and this demo reads the tiny color changes as blood pulses through your finger — then turns that one signal into seven live readings, each labeled honestly: what's a real measurement, what's a trend, and what's only an estimate.

Technique · Photoplethysmography (PPG) Sensor · Rear camera + light Data · Processed on this device — no upload

🔦 First: turn your flashlight ON

This is the single biggest upgrade to the reading. Safari and most browsers can't switch your phone's torch on for you — but if you turn it on yourself, it stays on while the demo reads, and the light shining through your fingertip is what makes the pulse signal strong and clean.

STEP 1

Flashlight on

Swipe into Control Center (iPhone) or Quick Settings (Android) and tap the flashlight so it's ON. On some Android Chrome browsers, the demo can switch the torch on automatically — it will try when you press Start.

STEP 2

Cover camera + flash

Place one fingertip gently over the rear camera. The flash sits right beside the lens, so one fingertip covers both. Press lightly — pressing hard squeezes the blood out and kills the signal.

STEP 3

Hold still, 30–60 sec

Sit down, rest your hand on a surface, and don't talk or shift your finger. Motion is the #1 enemy of this reading. Heart rate lands in ~10 seconds; the slower readings need the full minute.

No flashlight? It can still work in bright, even light — expect a weaker signal and a lower quality score below.

The seven readings

Every reading below updates live from the same fingertip signal. Under each one: what it is, whether it's accurate or an estimate, and exactly what would make it more accurate. No reading here is dressed up as more than it is.

Heart Rate

Measurement
— BPM
Waiting for a reading…

Beats per minute, measured from the pulse in your fingertip. This is the headline reading — the one this technique genuinely does well.

Accurate?Yes — at rest, usually within a few BPM of a pulse oximeter. This is a measurement, not an estimate.
More accurate withStill finger, gentle pressure, flashlight on, warm hands, a 30–60 second read, and averaging two reads back to back.

Live Pulse Waveform

Raw signal
—
The wave appears in the panel above once you start.

Your heartbeat drawn live, exactly as the camera sees it — each rise is blood surging into your fingertip, each fall is it draining away.

Accurate?It's the raw signal itself, not an estimate — what you see is what the camera captured.
Cleaner withA steady finger, even light through the fingertip, and no change in pressure mid-read. Motion shows up as jagged spikes — that's artifact, not your heart.

Heart Rate Variability (HRV)

Estimate · Trend
— MS · RMSSD
Needs ~15 steady beats to appear.

The variation in timing between one beat and the next. Higher, steadier variation generally tracks with recovery; a falling trend can track with stress or fatigue.

Accurate?Estimate — trend only. A camera samples ~30 times per second, so each beat's timing is only precise to ~33 ms. Too coarse for clinical-grade HRV.
More accurate withLonger reads (5 minutes beats 60 seconds), same time of day, fully at rest, still finger — and comparing your own trend across days instead of single numbers.

Breathing Rate

Estimate
— BREATHS / MIN
Needs ~45 seconds of clean signal.

Breaths per minute, inferred from the way your breathing subtly swells and fades the strength of your pulse signal.

Accurate?Estimate — approximate, not exact. It's pulled out of a side-effect on the pulse signal, not measured at your nose or chest.
More accurate withA longer, still read (60–90 seconds), breathing naturally rather than forcing it, and a clean pulse signal underneath.

Irregular Rhythm Flag

Flag · Not a diagnosis
—
Needs ~15 steady beats to judge spacing.

Watches the spacing between beats. If the gaps are noticeably uneven across a clean read, it raises a flag — nothing more.

Accurate?A flag, not a diagnosis and not an estimate of any condition. Motion and a shifting finger can fake uneven spacing, so a flag here proves nothing either way.
More reliable withLonger reads (more beats to judge), a motion-free signal, and repeat reads. Only an ECG or a clinician can confirm anything this flags — and this cannot detect or rule out any heart condition.

Blood Oxygen (SpO2)

Estimate only
~— %
Waiting for a stable signal…

An estimate of oxygen saturation worked out from how your fingertip absorbs the camera's color channels as blood pulses through.

Accurate?Estimate only — not a measurement. Real oximeters shine red and infrared light through the finger; a phone camera has no infrared, so this number can be off significantly, and accuracy varies across skin tones.
More accurate withStill finger, flashlight on, warm hands, averaged reads — but nothing in this app makes it a true measurement. Only a real pulse oximeter does that. Do not use this number for health decisions.

How it works

01 · LIGHT

Light through the finger

The flashlight shines through your fingertip. With every heartbeat, blood volume in the finger rises and falls — and so does how much light the camera picks back up.

02 · SIGNAL

Color becomes a wave

The demo samples the camera's red channel many times per second. That pulsing brightness is your pulse wave — the same PPG principle a watch or finger oximeter uses, through a camera instead of a dedicated sensor.

03 · READINGS

Peaks become numbers

Peaks in the wave are beats. Beat timing gives heart rate, HRV, and rhythm spacing; slow swells in the wave give breathing; color ratios feed the two labeled estimates. All processing happens on this device.
