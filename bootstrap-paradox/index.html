Bootstrap Paradox

Who made the watch?

Open the app →

What it does

An old stranger hands you a brass pocket watch. You travel forty years back in time and hand that same watch to your younger self. Then you return to the present and the loop closes.

So the question is simple. The watch was never made — it only ever changed hands. Somewhere between those two moments, somebody had to make it. Nobody did. Where did it come from?

The paradox is named after Robert A. Heinlein's 1941 story "By His Bootstraps," where a man becomes his own ancestor. Philosophers call it a causal loop: an object or a piece of information that is its own cause, with no origin. The app walks you through the loop yourself, then shows you why every answer to "who made the watch?" breaks.

How it works

  1. Become the travelerFour chapters: take the watch, travel back forty years, hand it over, return.
  2. Watch the loop closeThe timeline draws itself as you go, until the gold arc connects the end back to the beginning.
  3. Trace the originThe origin tracer walks the chain backward, link by link, and finds no first link. Then you vote: who made the watch?

Take the watch. Close the loop. Find the maker.
