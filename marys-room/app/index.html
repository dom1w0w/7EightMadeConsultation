Knowing about red is not knowing red.

Mary knew every physical fact — the wavelength, the cones, the brain. What she didn't have was what red is like.

That gap has a name: philosophers call the what-it's-like of an experience qualia. Jackson's argument is simple — if Mary learns something when she leaves the room, then physical facts alone don't capture everything about the mind.

Honest footnote: Jackson later changed his mind. He decided Mary gains a new ability — to recognize, imagine, remember red — not a new fact. The argument still stands as the sharpest test of whether the mind is more than the machine.
