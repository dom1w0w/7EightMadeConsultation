Mary's Room

She knows everything about color. She has never seen it.

Open the app →

What it does

Mary has lived her whole life in a black-and-white room. She has learned everything there is to know about color — the wavelengths, the retina, the brain — from books and lectures. She has never seen red.

The app puts you in the room with her. The screen itself is grayscale. You learn the facts with her. Then you leave the room — and the world floods with color. Did she learn something new?

The thought experiment comes from Frank Jackson, a philosopher who published it in 1982 as "Epiphenomenal Qualia." If Mary learns something when she leaves the room, then physical facts alone don't capture everything about the mind. The app ends exactly there, and explains the main answers people give — including Jackson's own change of mind.

If your phone allows it, you can use your camera as the room: the world itself stays grayscale until you leave it. The camera never leaves your device.

How it works

  1. Enter the roomThe whole screen is grayscale — the phone is the room. Optionally, your camera becomes the room instead.
  2. Learn the factsWavelength, cones, brain. Everything there is to know about red — with no color anywhere.
  3. Leave the roomColor floods in. Vote: did she learn something new? Then read the explanation — knowing vs. seeing.

Step into the room. Then decide what she learns.
