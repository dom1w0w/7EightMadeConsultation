Dichotomy Zeno

The gap does not close.

What it does

Halve the space between your thumb and index finger. Then halve what's left. Then do it again, forever. This is Zeno's dichotomy paradox, and Dichotomy Zeno lets you feel it with your own fingers.

The Studio app explains the paradox with pictures and has a halver you can press. Each press covers half of what's left, so the distance covered climbs toward the whole thing while the gap shrinks toward nothing without ever reaching it.

The pinch version, Dichotomy Zeno on dichotomy-zeno.grok.me, is free and runs in your browser. Pinch, slide, or halve the gap exactly in two. At the floor of each world it opens outward to the next one: outline, print, cell, atom. It’s in English and Japanese.

How it works

  1. Start with a pinchThe gap starts at 21 mm, about a relaxed pinch.
  2. Halve the gapEach press covers half of what's left. Or halve it 10 times at once.
  3. Keep going downAbout 28 halvings take you from your fingers to an atom. The gap still isn’t zero.

Halve it yourself. The gap never closes.
